How the 1–5 deception score is calculated

Method, and what this is not

Records enter polislop only with at least two independent, reputable sources, listed under each entry. The deception score is an editorial assessment against the rubric above — not a legal finding, and not a claim that any ad broke a law. Entries marked provisional have a rubric input that reporting has not yet settled; their scores may move.

The score grades how an ad depicts real people. It does not grade whether the ad's underlying political claims are true — that is fact-checking, and a separate job.

On embedded video

Each record's video area has three states, and never a fourth. Where the original ad has been located and its provenance established, it is framed in the page. Where the original exists but cannot be framed — a platform that does not play reliably inside another site, or a link nothing has yet tied to the ad — the card names the platform and links straight to it. Where the original has not been found, the card says so plainly rather than filling the space.

The ledger will not frame a video on the strength of a plausible-looking link. Every located original carries a provenance grade saying how it was established: Official account, published on the sponsor's own campaign or committee account; Archive, captured in a political-ad archive or public ad library; Reporting, where credible coverage identifies that exact posting as the ad; Human-reviewed, where a person watched the footage against the cited reporting. A link that meets none of these is marked Unverified and is never framed.

What the grades have in common is that each answers the question a player has to answer — is the thing on the other end of this link the advertisement? — from an independent direction. A news segment about an ad is never shown in place of the ad, and links to reporting are always labelled as reporting.

Ads play here from four platforms, each by that platform's own supported route: YouTube in a frame, X through its widget script, and Instagram and Facebook through Meta's oEmbed API — polislop asks Meta for the public post's embed markup and lets Meta's own script render it. Framing a Meta URL directly does not work, but that is a fact about iframes, not about Meta content: public Instagram and Facebook posts and reels are embeddable, and the ones here are embedded.

No provider is taken on trust, because none of them is reliable in the same way. X answers identical markup with a working post one minute and an empty shell the next. Meta's oembed_post and oembed_video endpoints return success for any URL without checking whether the post exists. So every one of these starts as the Watch original ad button, builds the player out of sight, and swaps the two only once the provider has actually rendered something. If a rendered post later collapses, the button comes back. The page would rather hand you a button that works than a rectangle that might.

One Meta link genuinely cannot be embedded: an Ad Library permalink is a viewer for an ad rather than a public post, and no oEmbed endpoint serves one. Those are linked, and so is anything Meta declines. A record only says the original was not located when no copy of the ad has been found at all — never merely because a platform refused to embed it.

Jurisdiction

FCC jurisdiction covers broadcast, cable, satellite and radio. Ads that ran only on social platforms or web video fall outside it, and the ledger says so per record rather than sending you to the wrong agency.
